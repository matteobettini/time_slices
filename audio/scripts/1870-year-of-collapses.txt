Eighteen Seventy. The year everything fell down.

In six weeks of summer, the second French Empire — Napoleon the Third's gilded reign — collapses at Sedan. One hundred thousand soldiers surrender. The emperor himself becomes a Prussian prisoner. Back in Paris, a republic is declared in two days. The empress flees to England.

But there's more falling to come.

On September twentieth, Italian Bersaglieri troops breach the ancient Aurelian Walls at Rome's Porta Pia. The Pope's temporal kingdom — a thousand years of earthly rule — ends in an afternoon. Pius the Ninth retreats to the Vatican and declares himself a prisoner. He will never leave those walls again.

Here's the paradox: just two months earlier, that same Pope had convened the First Vatican Council to declare papal infallibility — the doctrine that the Pope cannot err on matters of faith and morals. Absolute spiritual authority, proclaimed precisely as territorial sovereignty vanishes. The Vatican becomes a fortress of doctrine when it can no longer be a fortress of stone.

Meanwhile, the war scatters artists like seeds.

Monet flees to London with his wife and infant son. Through the winter fog, he paints the Thames beneath Westminster — Impressionism's first exile landscape. Pissarro arrives too, painting the Crystal Palace while learning that Prussian soldiers are using his canvases back home as butcher's aprons. Bazille, twenty-eight years old, is killed at Beaune-la-Rolande.

In Munich, Wagner's "Walküre" premieres against his will — King Ludwig refuses to wait for Bayreuth. The young Nietzsche, only twenty-five and already a Basel professor, serves as a medical orderly, contracting dysentery while tending dying soldiers in cattle cars. He's writing "The Birth of Tragedy," arguing that creation emerges from destruction.

By January eighteen seventy-one, the German Empire will be proclaimed at Versailles.

Three states die: the Second Empire, the Papal States, the German Confederation. Three are born: the Third Republic, the Kingdom of Italy with Rome, the German Empire. Europe's map redrawn in eight months.

And in London, refugees from the siege are building the networks that will transform modern art.

Collapse as creation. The theme of the age.
