In sixteen forty-one, everything solid begins to dissolve.

In Amsterdam, René Descartes publishes six meditations that dismantle the entire edifice of human knowledge. He sits alone, by the fire, and doubts. He doubts his senses — they have deceived him before. He doubts his memories — perhaps he is dreaming. He doubts mathematics itself — what if a malicious demon makes two plus three equal five only to torment him? He doubts everything except one stubborn fact: the very act of doubting proves something exists to doubt. Cogito ergo sum. I think, therefore I am.

From this single certainty, Descartes rebuilds philosophy. The Meditations on First Philosophy appears with objections from Hobbes, Gassendi, Arnauld — the most formidable minds of Europe attacking, and Descartes parrying. Philosophy becomes combat conducted in public.

In London, a different kind of dissolution. On the twelfth of May, the Earl of Strafford is beheaded on Tower Hill before a crowd of thousands. King Charles the First, unable to protect his most capable minister, signed the death warrant — the king's hand forced by Parliament, by fear, by the mob. The Grand Remonstrance passes in November by eleven votes, listing two hundred and four grievances against the crown. The Star Chamber, that engine of royal prerogative justice, is abolished. The Irish Rebellion erupts in October. Civil war is eighteen months away.

And in December, Anthony van Dyck dies in London at forty-two. He had painted the king and court with serene magnificence — hands elegant, fabrics luminous, power looking exactly as power wished to appear. That world is cracking around him.

In Venice, seventy-four-year-old Monteverdi stages Ulysses returning home after twenty years of wandering. The recognition scene builds on Penelope's aching disbelief — can this ragged stranger really be her husband? After everything that has been lost, can anything familiar remain?

In Rome, Claude Lorrain paints golden light dissolving architecture into atmosphere. In Amsterdam, Rembrandt paints Protestant faith as intimate conversation. Truth pursued through opposite means — classical idealization and psychological depth — yet both artists refuse inherited formulas for something seen, something felt.

The age of inherited authority is ending. Descartes' doubt and England's crisis enact the same dissolution: whether scholastic or monarchical, power that once commanded assent must now justify itself from first principles.

Everything must be rebuilt from the ground up. Some will find the cogito. Others will find the scaffold.
