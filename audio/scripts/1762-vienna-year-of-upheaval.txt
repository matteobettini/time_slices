Seventeen sixty-two. The year authority cracked.

In April, Jean-Jacques Rousseau publishes two books that will change everything. The Social Contract opens with the most dangerous sentence in Enlightenment philosophy: "Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains." Within weeks, both Paris and Geneva have banned it. Copies burn in public squares. Rousseau flees to Switzerland, a wanted man.

But his idea cannot be burned. Sovereignty belongs to the people, he argues — the general will, not divine right. Kings are merely servants who can be dismissed. Robespierre will keep this book by his bed. Jefferson will read it as he drafts a declaration.

In Vienna that October, a different revolution unfolds. Christoph Willibald Gluck premieres Orfeo ed Euridice before Empress Maria Theresa. He has stripped opera of its Baroque excesses — the endless virtuoso arias, the convoluted plots. What remains is what he calls "noble simplicity." When Orpheus sings "Che farò senza Euridice?" — "What shall I do without Euridice?" — the melody achieves its power through restraint. Opera will never be the same.

Meanwhile, empires are reshuffling. In January, the Russian Empress Elisabeth dies. Her nephew Peter the Third immediately makes peace with Prussia — the "Miracle of Brandenburg" that saves Frederick the Great from defeat. But Peter reigns only six months. His German wife orchestrates a palace coup, and Catherine the Great ascends the throne. She will rule for thirty-four years, corresponding with Voltaire about liberty while expanding serfdom to millions.

Britain captures Havana from Spain in August, Manila in October. The Seven Years' War is ending, and the map of three continents is being redrawn.

And in Toulouse, a Protestant merchant named Jean Calas is broken on the wheel for a murder he did not commit. Voltaire investigates, is outraged, and begins a campaign that will produce his Treatise on Tolerance — and transform the intellectual into the conscience of power.

Seventeen sixty-two: the year authority stopped being given and had to be earned.
