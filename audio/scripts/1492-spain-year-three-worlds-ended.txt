Fourteen ninety-two. The year three worlds ended.

On January second, Boabdil, the last sultan of Granada, hands over the keys to the city. Ferdinand and Isabella ride through the gates of the Alhambra, that impossible palace of light and water and geometric perfection. Eight centuries of Islamic Spain — the caliphate of Córdoba, the libraries, the translators, the poetry — vanish into Christian hands.

Three months later, the Alhambra Decree. All Jews must convert or leave by July thirty-first. Some choose baptism. Tens of thousands choose exile — to Portugal, to Italy, to the Ottoman Empire, which welcomes them. They carry with them the Sephardic tradition: Hebrew poetry, philosophical commentaries, translations from Arabic that had enriched all of Europe. Spain becomes poorer, more uniform, more brutal.

Then August. Columbus sails from Palos with three small ships. The harbor, they say, was clogged with refugee vessels carrying the last Jews to exile. His voyage is funded partly by confiscated Jewish property, partly by a converso financier named Luis de Santángel. On October twelfth, he lands in the Bahamas, convinced he's reached Asia. A hemisphere opens — and millions will die for it.

Meanwhile in Florence, Lorenzo de' Medici has died in April. The golden age of Neoplatonic humanism, the Platonic Academy of Ficino and Poliziano and the young Pico della Mirandola — suddenly orphaned. Pico had proclaimed humanity infinitely perfectible. Now his patron is gone, and he turns to Savonarola's apocalyptic visions.

And on August eleventh, in Rome, Rodrigo Borgia becomes Pope Alexander the Sixth. The most corrupt pope of the Renaissance begins his reign just as Columbus crosses the ocean.

These aren't coincidences. Columbus was at the surrender of Granada. His voyage was authorized in Santa Fe, the same camp where the surrender terms were negotiated. Nebrija's grammar of Castilian — the first of any modern European language — was published the same year, presented to Isabella with the argument that language accompanies empire.

Fourteen ninety-two. The convivencia dies. The Sephardic diaspora begins. A new hemisphere opens for conquest. Medieval Iberia ends, and something harder, vaster, more violent is born.

The year three worlds ended — and one began.
