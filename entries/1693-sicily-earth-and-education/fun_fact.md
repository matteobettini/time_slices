Locke's advice on education included some startlingly specific recommendations. To toughen children against cold, he suggested their feet "be washed every day in cold water, and to have his shoes so thin that they might leak and let in water whenever he comes near it." The theory: if constantly exposed to wet feet, sudden rain wouldn't cause chills. This advice was widely followed among English gentry for generations — appearing in children's books throughout the eighteenth century — though whether it produced hardy constitutions or chronic respiratory infections remains unclear. The philosopher who argued minds arrive blank apparently believed feet should arrive soggy.
