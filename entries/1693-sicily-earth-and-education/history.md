The **Nine Years' War** grinds on — the great coalition of England, the Dutch Republic, Spain, the Holy Roman Empire, and Savoy against **Louis XIV's** France. In June, the **Battle of Lagos** off Portugal sees the French Mediterranean fleet defeat the combined Anglo-Dutch convoy, capturing a fortune in merchant shipping. In July, the **Battle of Landen** in the Spanish Netherlands becomes one of the war's bloodiest encounters: William III's coalition forces lose 19,000 men against French General Luxembourg. French tactical dominance is clear, but Louis cannot convert battlefield success into strategic victory; the coalition keeps forming, keeps fighting, fueled by William III's stubborn determination and Dutch commercial wealth.

Sicily — where the earthquake struck — belongs to **Spain**, part of the vast Habsburg inheritance now governed from Madrid by the sickly **Charles II**, whose lack of heirs will trigger the War of Spanish Succession within the decade. The earthquake relief effort reveals the apparatus of early-modern governance: the Viceroy dispatches commissioners, architects are summoned, rebuilding plans are drawn. Spanish colonial administration, for all its corruption, proves capable of coordinating the reconstruction that will create Sicilian Baroque.

In England, **William III** and **Mary II** rule jointly following the **Glorious Revolution** of 1688 — the constitutional monarchy that subordinated crown to Parliament. The war dominates: taxation, naval appropriations, the new **Bank of England** (founded 1694 to fund war debt). In April, **Tituba** — enslaved woman convicted at Salem — is released from jail after thirteen months when someone pays her fees. The witch trial hysteria has passed, leaving twenty dead and a society questioning how spectral evidence ever found its way into courts.

The **College of William & Mary** receives its royal charter in Virginia — the first institution of higher education in the colonial South, and only the second in British North America after Harvard. **Anne Palles** becomes the last person executed for witchcraft in Denmark, beheaded rather than burned alive at her own request. The **Order of Saint Louis** — France's first military decoration open to commoners — is established. **John Harrison**, future inventor of the marine chronometer that will solve the longitude problem, is born in Yorkshire.

In Africa, the **Kingdom of Kongo** spirals through political violence; King Manuel I is executed by his successor. The Maratha Empire fights the Mughals in India. China restricts foreign trade to Canton. The Amish sect forms through schism within Swiss Anabaptist communities. The world turns toward modernity, but unevenly, violently, catastrophically.
