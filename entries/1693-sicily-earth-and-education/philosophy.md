**John Locke** publishes *Some Thoughts Concerning Education*, and the implications are vast. Building on the *tabula rasa* theory from his *Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689), Locke argues that children arrive with minds like blank slates — no innate ideas, no predetermined virtue or vice. "Nine parts of ten of what men are, good or evil, useful or not, comes from their education." This is philosophical dynamite. If minds are blank, then education — not birth, not original sin, not inherited status — determines human character. The implications for **social reform**, for the possibility of **human improvement**, for the challenge to **aristocratic privilege** based on blood, are revolutionary.

Locke's pedagogical advice is practical: hardy physical regimens (cold baths, thin shoes), reasoning with children rather than merely commanding them, developing habits of virtue rather than memorizing rules. But beneath the specifics lies a new vision of humanity as infinitely malleable through nurture. **Rousseau** would build on this in *Émile*; every modern debate about nature versus nurture traces to this treatise.

**Edmond Halley** — who had funded Newton's *Principia* from his own pocket six years earlier — applies mathematical rigor to mortality itself. Using **demographic records from Breslau**, he constructs the first **scientific life table**: a mathematical model correlating age with probability of death. Halley uses this to calculate the pricing of **life annuities** — transforming speculation about lifespan into actuarial calculation. Insurance, pensions, demography: entire industries trace their mathematical foundations to Halley's 1693 paper in the *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society*. Death becomes calculable.

**William Penn**, Quaker founder of Pennsylvania, publishes *An Essay towards the Present and Future Peace of Europe*, proposing a **European Parliament** — an assembly of nations that could arbitrate disputes and prevent war. It's utopian, visionary, and ahead of its time by centuries. Penn imagines representative voting by population, regular sessions, enforcement mechanisms. The **League of Nations**, the **European Union** — the dream of cooperative internationalism begins here, in a Quaker's pamphlet, while the **Nine Years' War** rages across the continent.

The year also sees **Cotton Mather** publish *Wonders of the Invisible World* — his defense of the **Salem witch trials** that had only just concluded. An artifact of the old worldview: spectral evidence, satanic conspiracy, invisible forces at war. That Locke's *tabula rasa* and Mather's demonology coexist in the same year measures the philosophical chasm opening in late-seventeenth-century thought.
