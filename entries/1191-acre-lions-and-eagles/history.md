On **July 12, 1191**, after two years of siege, **Acre** falls to the Crusaders. **Philip II of France** and **Richard I of England** — the Lionheart — have joined the siege begun by Guy of Lusignan in 1189. **Saladin** watches from the hills, unable to break the Crusader lines. The surrender terms include prisoner exchanges and a massive ransom; when Saladin delays payment, Richard orders **3,000 Muslim prisoners executed** on August 20 — women and children among them. The massacre at Ayyadieh poisons the well; Saladin executes his Latin captives in response. Holy war hardens into atrocity.

On **September 7**, the **Battle of Arsuf**: Richard's army, marching south from Acre toward Jaffa, defeats Saladin's cavalry in open battle. Richard maintains iron discipline — his column a moving fortress, infantry screening cavalry, crossbowmen devastating Muslim horse archers. When the Hospitallers finally break ranks to charge, Richard commits his full force at the perfect moment. The Crusaders retake the central Palestinian coast. But Jerusalem remains beyond reach.

Before arriving, Richard conquered **Cyprus** in May — seizing it from Isaac Komnenos in less than a month, creating a Crusader kingdom that will outlast all the others. He married **Berengaria of Navarre** on the island on May 12. Philip, citing illness and quarrels with Richard, departs for France on July 31, leaving the English king in sole command — and plotting with Richard's brother John to seize his continental lands.

Meanwhile, in **Germany**, Emperor **Frederick Barbarossa** is dead — drowned crossing the Saleph River in Anatolia the previous year, his great army dissolving before reaching the Holy Land. The **Third Crusade** is the kings' crusade, but one king is dead, another has gone home, and the third cannot take Jerusalem.
